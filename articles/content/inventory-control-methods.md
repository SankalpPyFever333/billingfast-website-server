# Inventory Control Methods - In-depth Guide With Examples

**Learn the essential techniques for managing your inventory efficiently and
improving your business cash flow.**

![Inventory Control](/assets/images/inventory-control/header.png)

## Why Is It Essential To Implement Inventory Control Methods?

Excess inventory ties up cash and warehouse space that could be better utilized
elsewhere. Implementing proper inventory control methods helps you:

### Avoid Spoilage

If you have products that get spoilt or expired, then you have to keep track
that you sell them on time. Proper inventory control ensures older stock moves
first.

### Prevent Dead Stock

Dead stock refers to obsolete products that can no longer be sold due to
changing trends, seasons, or market demands. Good inventory control helps
identify and reduce dead stock.

### Reduce Storage Costs

Excess inventory means higher warehousing expenses. By maintaining optimal stock
levels, you minimize storage costs significantly.

### Improve Cash Flow

Inventory purchased represents tied-up capital until sold. Better inventory
control means faster turnover and improved cash flow.

## 8 Techniques for Inventory Management and Control

### 1. Setting Par Levels

![Par Levels](/assets/images/inventory-control/par-levels.png)

Establish minimum stock thresholds that trigger reordering. Par levels should be
based on:

- Historical sales trends
- Lead time from suppliers
- Seasonal demand fluctuations
- Safety stock requirements

When inventory drops below the par level, it's time to order more.

### 2. FIFO Principle (First In, First Out)

![FIFO](/assets/images/inventory-control/fifo.png)

The FIFO principle ensures oldest stock sells first. This is critical for:

- Perishable goods
- Products with expiry dates
- Seasonal items
- Fashion and trend-based products

Always arrange your warehouse so older stock is more accessible for picking.

### 3. Regular Inventory Auditing

Conduct regular audits to ensure your records match actual stock. Three
approaches:

**Physical Inventory** Complete count of all inventory, typically done annually.

**Spot Checking** Random product verification throughout the year to catch
discrepancies early.

**Cycle Counting** Rotating daily or weekly audits of different product
categories.

### 4. Contingency Planning

Prepare for unexpected situations:

- **Overselling**: What happens when you sell more than you have?
- **Overstocking**: How do you handle excess inventory?
- **Manufacturer Shortages**: Backup suppliers and alternative products
- **Calculation Errors**: Systems for catching and correcting mistakes

### 5. Prioritize Top Moving Items (ABC Analysis)

![ABC Analysis](/assets/images/inventory-control/abc-analysis.png)

Categorize products by value and sales frequency:

- **A Items**: High-value products that need tight control
- **B Items**: Medium-value products with moderate attention
- **C Items**: Low-value products with simple controls

Focus your attention and resources on the products that matter most.

### 6. Consider Drop Shipping

Transfer inventory responsibility to manufacturers or wholesalers:

- Reduce warehouse space requirements
- Lower inventory carrying costs
- Expand product offerings without risk
- Focus on marketing and customer service

### 7. Forecast Accurately

![Forecasting](/assets/images/inventory-control/forecasting.png)

Predict demand using multiple data sources:

- **Market Trends**: Industry reports and competitor analysis
- **Historical Sales**: Past performance patterns
- **Seasonality**: Annual cycles and peak periods
- **Marketing Data**: Planned promotions and campaigns

Better forecasting means less overstock and fewer stockouts.

### 8. Manage Supplier Relationships

Strong supplier communication helps with:

- Faster restocking during high demand
- Better terms for returns and exchanges
- Early warning about supply issues
- Negotiating better prices

Build relationships with multiple suppliers to reduce dependency risks.
